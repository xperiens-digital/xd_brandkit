# Imagery

X.D uses generated ambient textures in the V1 palette instead of stock photography. They were made for X.D (5 Nov 2026), so X.D owns them outright: no licence, credit or expiry, and any of them can be regenerated at any size or in any service colour family. The selection board is "X.D Ambient Imagery Directions".

## Where each texture goes

| Use | Texture | Files (Imagery asset group) |
|---|---|---|
| The five service pages and service cards (locked) | Grain mesh, one colourway per service | `service-mesh-<reengineer|intel|cloud|govern|innovate>.webp`, `service-mesh-hero.webp` |
| General hero banners and section heroes | Six combinations: dot bloom, dithered horizon, pixel dissolve, dot matrix, dot drift, halftone orbit | `hero-<name>.webp` (1600 × 900) |
| Insights article headers and listing cards | The same six, in three colourways | `insight-<name>-<1|2|3>.webp` (1600 × 600): 1 warm, 2 cool, 3 lavender |

The combinations are built from four base textures: 01 paper and dither, 02 pixel field, 03 grain mesh and 05 halftone, plus new dot patterns.

## Rules

- Colour mass sits on the right; the left stays calm. Headlines, breadcrumbs, chips and bylines always sit on the calm side and pass 4.5:1 against it.
- One texture per page. Vary textures between neighbouring pages and articles.
- Service pages always use grain mesh in their own colour family; never another service's colourway.
- Textures are decorative: empty `alt`, with the meaning carried by the heading.
- Never put people, logos, screens or other brands' imagery in or over a texture. Never recolour a texture outside the V1 families.
- Deliver WebP for the web and PNG for documents. Ask for a new variation rather than stretching or cropping one past 60% of its width.

## Sources beyond these

Generated textures are the default. For a one-off shape or pattern, free generators such as Haikei and fffuel can be set to the V1 hex values (check each tool's current licence). For a real-world texture (frosted glass, light refraction, paper macro), Unsplash and Pexels allow free commercial use without attribution; grade toward the V1 hues and keep faces, logos and screens out.
